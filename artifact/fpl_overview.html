A published page cannot call the Fantasy Premier League API itself — the viewer sandbox blocks requests to outside servers. Refreshing takes one command in the project:

python refresh_artifact.py

That calls the API, recalculates every fixture-difficulty figure on this page and rewrites it. Publish the page again afterwards and this link shows the new numbers.

Every figure on this page comes from the live Fantasy Premier League feed — the 2026/27 season as it stands after 90 matches, 9 gameweeks in.

What it is

A pick process, not a tip sheet

Most Fantasy Premier League tools hand you a ranked list and leave you to trust it. This one walks you through the decision instead: first choose the teams worth buying from, then the players inside them, then the shape, then the transfers. Each step narrows the field and passes its shortlist to the next, so by the time you reach a transfer you can still see every reason it was proposed.

The application refreshes itself from the official Fantasy Premier League feed each time you open it, so form, prices, injuries and fixtures are always the live ones. Nothing is hand-maintained.

Where it stands

Two things to finish

Seven of the nine pages are built and wired to each other, and two tabs beyond the specification — a browser over every player in the game, and a watchlist — sit at the end of the bar. What is left is one unbuilt page and two places where the tool is quieter than the specification asks.

  1. 01

    The efficiency page itself is not built

    Not built

    Step 8, where teams are ranked by efficiency and their next five and ten fixtures are re-rated, is still a placeholder. Every number it needs already exists in the application — the table above was produced from the project's own data — so this is a page to assemble, not a calculation to invent.

    SettledThe efficiency rating weights recent matches more heavily: a team's last five matches count triple an earlier one. That decision is already reflected in every number on this page, so the page can be built against these figures directly.

  2. 02

    Two rules are applied but not shown

    Small gaps

    The D marker for the eight meanest defences currently appears on the current-team page only, though the specification asks for it in every view where a player is listed. And the transfers page proposes changes without printing the six principles it used to reach them — the reasoning is sound, it is simply invisible at the moment a manager is deciding whether to trust it.

  3. 03

    The analysis scripts now meet a part-played season

    Worth knowing

    The stored data is no longer a finished season. The standalone analysis scripts read whatever sits in the data folder as the current season, so anything that assumes thirty-eight rounds — the best-team-per-gameweek study, the manager comparisons — is now working with nine. Last season has not been lost: it moved to an archive folder beside it, and a script that wants a complete season should be pointed there.

The rules

Six principles the tool applies everywhere

These are the judgements built into every ranking and every transfer proposal — the house style of the pick process.

  1. Form over fixtures

    What a player is doing right now outranks who they are about to play.

  2. Never pick an injured player

    Injuries, doubts and suspensions are flagged wherever a player appears.

  3. Defenders from mean defences

    Players from the eight teams conceding fewest goals are marked D.

  4. Attacking midfielders

The idea behind the numbers

Difficulty is only half the story

Every fixture arrives with a difficulty rating from 1 (easy) to 5 (hard). On its own that says nothing about whether a team actually takes advantage of an easy run — so the tool measures that too.

Step 1

points per match ÷ average difficulty

An efficiency rating: how many league points a team squeezes out of each unit of fixture difficulty. High means a team makes its fixtures count. Each team's last five matches count triple, so the rating follows current form rather than treating August as evidence about next Saturday.

Step 2

fixture difficulty ÷ efficiency rating

An adjusted difficulty: the same fixture is harder for a team that wastes its chances and easier for one that converts them.
