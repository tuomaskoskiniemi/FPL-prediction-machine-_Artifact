What it is

A pick process, not a tip sheet

Most Fantasy Premier League tools hand you a ranked list and leave you to trust it. This one walks you through the decision instead: first choose the teams worth buying from, then the players inside them, then the shape, then the transfers. Each step narrows the field and passes its shortlist to the next, so by the time you reach a transfer you can still see every reason it was proposed.

The application refreshes itself from the official Fantasy Premier League feed each time you open it, so form, prices, injuries and fixtures are always the live ones. Nothing is hand-maintained.

The rules

Six principles the tool applies everywhere

These are the judgements built into every ranking and every transfer proposal — the house style of the pick process.

  1. Form over fixtures

    What a player is doing right now outranks who they are about to play.

  2. Never pick an injured player

    Injuries, doubts and suspensions are flagged wherever a player appears.

  3. Defenders from mean defences

    Players from the eight teams conceding fewest goals are marked D.

  4. Attacking midfielders

    Midfielders are judged on goals and assists, not just on appearances.

  5. Attacking defenders

    A defender who creates and scores is worth more than a clean-sheet specialist.

  6. Players who play the full match

    Minutes are treated as a first-class statistic, not a footnote.

83 players currently carry an injury, doubt or suspension flag and are held back from proposals. The eight meanest defences so far are ARS, BHA, LEE, LIV, BRE, EVE, MCI, TOT.

The idea behind the numbers

Difficulty is only half the story

Every fixture arrives with a difficulty rating from 1 (easy) to 5 (hard). On its own that says nothing about whether a team actually takes advantage of an easy run — so the tool measures that too.

Step 1

points per match ÷ average difficulty

An efficiency rating: how many league points a team squeezes out of each unit of fixture difficulty. High means a team makes its fixtures count. Each team's last five matches count triple, so the rating follows current form rather than treating August as evidence about next Saturday.

Step 2

fixture difficulty ÷ efficiency rating

An adjusted difficulty: the same fixture is harder for a team that wastes its chances and easier for one that converts them.
